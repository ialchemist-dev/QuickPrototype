# ATTENTION RETURNED
## Scene 01 — The Morning Briefing

**Revision:** 2 — owner-directed creative revision, 2026-10-18.
**Status:** complete scene draft for review; not approved for generation.
**Language:** English. **Target length:** approximately 75 seconds, subject to a natural-voice timing pass.
**Location:** a modest contemporary apartment, early morning.
**Format:** cinematic live-action realism; 16:9; 24 fps.

### Dramatic intention

An ordinary person has a secretary who knows their life, prepares the details, and brings only the decisions that need their attention. Breakfast continues while important unfinished business becomes manageable. The emotional movement is responsibility → anticipation → affection → ease.

### Current creative decisions

- The protagonist wakes, prepares a simple breakfast, and summons the secretary with a finger snap while seated at the table.
- The secretary is a visible, humanlike virtual projection holding a slim virtual appointment book. This explicitly replaces the earlier voice-only/invisible-assistant direction.
- The secretary appears at conversational distance on the opposite side of the table: eye line at seated height, restrained translucency, natural expressions, simple contemporary clothing. No floating dashboards, menus, search results or glowing data clouds.
- The secretary and appointment book are projections. Neither can touch, move or arrange physical objects. Connected devices can carry out authorized digital instructions: the printer prints; the human retrieves the pages.
- This scene covers license renewal, a weekend hotel booking and an aunt's birthday card. Grocery replenishment and a hiking invitation/calendar conflict remain earlier development ideas, reserved for possible later scenes rather than added to this breakfast.
- Protagonist casting, gender and exact appearance remain open. The previously proposed Alex design is an option, not an owner-approved casting decision. The secretary's casting and final rendering style also remain subject to reference approval.

### Shot-by-shot script

Dialogue is spoken naturally in English. Timings are editorial targets, not tested final voice durations. A line may continue over the next image; camera changes must not force unnatural pauses.

#### S1-01 | 00:00–00:05 | A day begins

**Picture:** Two brief, quiet details: the protagonist puts their feet on the floor beside the bed; cut to toast and eggs being placed on a plate in the kitchen. The home is comfortable and lived-in, not a showroom.

**Camera:** Locked 50 mm details, matched by the hand movement into the breakfast preparation. These are two editorial inserts, not a single generated scene transition.

**Sound:** Bed linen, a pan's soft sizzle, a plate settling on the counter. No dialogue, no alarm montage.

#### S1-02 | 00:05–00:09 | An ordinary gesture

**Picture:** The protagonist settles at the breakfast table, sets down their mug, and casually snaps their fingers once. Across the table, the virtual secretary resolves gently into view with an appointment book already in hand. The gesture is familiar; neither character performs amazement.

**Camera:** Eye-level medium-wide, 35 mm; a very slight push toward the table. Keep the secretary clear of physical furniture and objects.

**Sound:** One dry, natural finger snap. No futuristic activation sound.

#### S1-03 | 00:09–00:12 | The briefing

**Picture:** The secretary makes brief eye contact and opens the appointment book. The protagonist begins eating.

**Camera:** Medium close-up of secretary, 50 mm. Relaxed posture; composed, familiar manner.

**SECRETARY:** “Morning. Three things to check with you.”

#### S1-04 | 00:12–00:20 | Already prepared

**Picture:** Start on the protagonist listening while cutting toast. Cut to the nearby printer: the final page slides into the output tray, joining the printed paperwork. Return to the breakfast table if the line allows. No one has moved the pages into a folder.

**Camera:** Medium over-shoulder on the table, then a locked 50 mm printer insert. Documents remain unreadable; no personal data or official agency marks.

**SECRETARY, continuing over the printer:** “Your driver's license expires next month. I've printed the renewal paperwork. You can grab it from the printer before you leave.”

**Sound:** Soft printer mechanism under the voice. The protagonist glances toward it, then returns to breakfast.

#### S1-05 | 00:20–00:27 | One small decision

**Picture:** The secretary checks the next line in the book. The protagonist answers without putting down their fork.

**Camera:** Comfortable two-shot, 35 mm, both at conversational eye level.

**SECRETARY:** “The earliest appointment is Wednesday at nine. Your calendar's clear. Shall I book it?”

**PROTAGONIST:** “Yes. Book it.”

**Performance:** The secretary acknowledges with a small nod and a mark in the virtual book. No claim that the appointment is confirmed before a booking result exists.

#### S1-06 | 00:27–00:39 | Something to look forward to

**Picture:** The secretary turns a page. As the trip comes up, the protagonist relaxes and looks up with genuine interest. Stay in the apartment; no hotel advertisement or destination montage interrupts the exchange.

**Camera:** Medium secretary angle into a gentle reverse on the protagonist, natural 50 mm perspective. The cut follows the listener's interest.

**SECRETARY:** “For next weekend's coast trip, Harbor House looks like your kind of place: quiet, with a balcony. Two nights, four twenty including tax. Free cancellation until Thursday.”

**Sound:** A restrained, warm musical phrase may enter here, well below dialogue.

#### S1-07 | 00:39–00:44 | She has checked the detail

**Picture:** The protagonist lifts the mug, pausing before drinking. The secretary answers immediately and simply.

**Camera:** Medium two-shot, nearly still. The exchange feels practiced, not rehearsed.

**PROTAGONIST:** “What about parking?”

**SECRETARY:** “Included.”

**PROTAGONIST:** “Great. Book that one.”

**Performance:** A nod and a small note in the book acknowledge the instruction. No payment interface, card details, hotel confirmation graphic or invented success notification.

#### S1-08 | 00:44–00:46 | Room to breathe

**Picture:** The protagonist takes the sip. The secretary waits, then moves to the final item. Allow a little shared silence.

**Camera:** Quiet insert of the mug returning to the table, 50 mm.

**Sound:** Ceramic on wood, a soft virtual page turn if used, morning room tone. No dialogue.

#### S1-09 | 00:46–00:52 | Someone who matters

**Picture:** The secretary looks up from the book. Her delivery is gently personal without becoming sentimental.

**Camera:** Medium close-up on secretary, then hold the protagonist's reaction.

**SECRETARY:** “And Aunt Linda's birthday is in two weeks. Shall we send a card?”

#### S1-10 | 00:52–00:57 | A remembered preference

**Picture:** A small, unforced smile. The protagonist briefly thinks of their aunt rather than of the assistant.

**Camera:** Close-up, 50 mm, natural skin texture and soft window light.

**PROTAGONIST:** “Yes. Something with flowers. She'd like that.”

#### S1-11 | 00:57–01:04 | The human part

**Picture:** The secretary offers help. The protagonist makes a gentle distinction: the organization can be delegated, but this message is theirs to write.

**Camera:** Begin on the secretary; cut to the protagonist for the answer. No emphatic musical cue.

**SECRETARY:** “Want me to draft a message?”

**PROTAGONIST:** “I'll write it. Remind me tonight.”

**Performance:** Warm and matter-of-fact, never a rebuke.

#### S1-12 | 01:04–01:09 | Ready, not sent

**Picture:** The secretary records the evening reminder. Nothing physical appears or is delivered. The card and personal message are still pending.

**Camera:** Medium close-up showing the virtual book and the secretary's face; no readable checklist is needed.

**SECRETARY:** “Of course. I'll have it ready for your review before it's sent.”

**Intent:** Prepare the floral-card option and reminder; after the protagonist writes the message, obtain final review before dispatch. Do not imply a completed purchase or mailing in this scene.

#### S1-13 | 01:09–01:12 | The list is closed

**Picture:** The secretary closes the appointment book. The protagonist gives a brief, appreciative look.

**Camera:** Return to the original table two-shot.

**SECRETARY:** “That's everything.”

**PROTAGONIST:** “Thanks.”

#### S1-14 | 01:12–01:15 | Breakfast continues

**Picture:** The projection fades quietly. The protagonist finishes a bite and looks toward the sunlit window. Hold on the person, not the empty projection space. The printed pages remain in the printer tray for the human to collect later.

**Camera:** Restrained wide, 35 mm; no dramatic reveal.

**Sound:** Morning room tone and the low musical phrase continue into the next scene. No title card here.

### Performance and visual rules

- The protagonist keeps eating, drinking, thinking and occasionally looking up. They do not maintain a fixed gaze at the projection throughout the briefing.
- Secretary delivery is informed, concise and respectful: a capable familiar colleague, not a sales presenter, chatbot, servant or omniscient narrator.
- The virtual book is a readable behavioral motif—open, consult, note, close—not a legible software UI. Keep its contents indistinct.
- Warm natural window light and restrained 35/50 mm camera language remain the visual foundation. Projection effects should be subtle enough for the scene to feel domestic.
- Visible speaking faces require reliable lip synchronization. If generation cannot achieve it, use listening reactions, printer/mug inserts or over-shoulder coverage while preserving the dialogue; do not silently deliver mismatched mouths.
- Preserve explicit approval before the appointment and hotel booking. Card dispatch follows the protagonist's personal message and final review.
